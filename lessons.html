Why it matters

The mouth faces 229°; the winter sun sets at 241°. A cone of mirrors pointed at the low sun bounces light across itself and focuses it toward the axis.

Over a year: about 550 hours with a spot at head height at 1× sun or more, peaking at 2.2× in late December. There's also a 3.8× line on the pad in early November and early February, when the sun lines up with the axis.

It all lands within about 11 ft of the pad: inside the funnel and just past the throat, where people were invited to walk through.

NOAA's Yerba Buena Island gauge has no wind sensor, and its harbour stations read 25–45% below the island. The island record is bridged to 2021–25 through Oakland airport.

Why it matters

The 175 sq ft mouth is a scoop turned 18–41° off the island's prevailing west and west-southwest winds. SF's walking-comfort yardstick is 11 mph, exceeded in no more than 10% of daytime hours. The open lawn already exceeds it in 24–36% of them; in the throat that rises to 34–64%.

These are real 2D flow solutions (lattice Boltzmann) on two slices cut from the exact funnel. Each slice lets air escape only one way (around the sides, or over the top), and the solver's low Reynolds number smooths the flow, so treat them as a guide. Settled, they give 1.3–1.4× in the throat and up to ~2× around the rim edges. Published wind-concentrator studies report 1–2.6×, mostly from simulations, so I use 1.2–1.5× for the throat.

A full 3D simulation (OpenFOAM, six wind directions) puts the force coefficient at about 1.5, highest for the ordinary west wind, acting 8 ft up with uplift of about half the horizontal force.

Why it matters

ADA 307.4 requires 80 in of headroom on a route, or a cane-detectable barrier. 307.2 allows objects between 27 and 80 in to overhang the walking line by 4 in at most. The throat's top is 78 in, and its rim curves in over the floor line: 4 in at 74 in, 9 in at 76 in and 22 in at 78 in.

The shape also invites climbing (a 31° start inside the throat, a 43° ridge up to 14 ft) and skating (a steel bowl on a smooth pad).

Why it matters

Treasure Island is hydraulic fill from 1936–37. In 1989 it produced sand boils, settlement and a lateral spread. A 14 ft piece with a 7 ft wind lever on a surface slab can tilt in a quake and keep settling after.

New building floors go at 12.7 ft NAVD88 or higher, for 36 in of sea level rise plus freeboard. Cityside Park was raised and now sits about 0.7 ft above the 100-year still water plus 36 in; adapting it further is triggered once the Bay rises 30 in. The park is 300 ft wide partly to leave room for that work. A permanent foundation there needs coordinating with TIDA.

All elevations are NAVD88 (TI Infrastructure Plan 2011, USGS lidar). Soil depths are island-wide ranges; no boring log for the park was found.

Why it matters

Stainless tea-stains where chlorides sit and nothing washes them away. Because the funnel's top panel covers its whole depth, vertical rain never reaches any of the inside, where the mirror finish matters most. Only storm rain blown into the mouth reaches it.

Needs 316L or duplex stainless, AAMA 2605 or porcelain enamel colours, isolated fasteners and a washing schedule.
