What Deep Listening didn't account for

Deep Listening was our proposal for a 14 ft sculpture of mirror and rainbow panels, with an 18 ft mouth, in Cityside Park. It didn't win the commission. These six studies, built from the proposal drawings and measured site data, show what the design missed.

Why it matters

The mouth faces 229° and the winter sun sets at 241°. A cone of mirrors pointed at the low sun bounces light across itself and focuses it toward the axis.

Over a year that means about 550 hours with a spot at head height of 1× sun or more, peaking at 2.2× in late December. In early November and early February the sun lines up with the axis and puts a 3.8× line on the pad.

It all lands within about 11 ft of the pad: inside the sculpture and just past the throat, where the proposal invited people to walk through.

NOAA's Yerba Buena Island gauge has no wind sensor, and its harbour stations read 25–43% below the island. The island record (1983–96, with gaps) is carried to 2021–Aug 2025 through Oakland airport.

Why it matters

The 175 sq ft mouth is a scoop turned 18–41° off the island's prevailing west and west-southwest winds. San Francisco's walking-comfort yardstick is 11 mph, exceeded in no more than 10% of daytime hours. The open lawn already exceeds it in 24–36% of them; in the throat that rises to 34–64%.

The two slices are real 2D flow solutions (lattice Boltzmann) cut from the exact shape. Each lets air escape only one way, so treat them as a guide. They settle at 1.3–1.4× in the throat and about 2× around the rim edges, so I use 1.2–1.5× for the throat.

A full 3D simulation (OpenFOAM, six wind directions) puts the force coefficient at about 1.5, highest for the ordinary west wind, acting 8 ft up with uplift of about half the horizontal force.

Why it matters

ADA 307.4 requires 80 in of headroom on a route, or a cane-detectable barrier. ADA 307.2 lets objects between 27 and 80 in overhang the walking line by 4 in at most. The throat's top is 78 in, and its rim curves in over the floor line: 4 in at 74 in, 9 in at 76 in and 22 in at 78 in.

The shape also invites climbing (an inner slope of about 40° at the throat, a 43° ridge up to 14 ft) and skating (a steel bowl on a smooth pad).

Why it matters

Treasure Island is hydraulic fill placed in 1936–37. In 1989 it produced sand boils, settlement and a lateral spread. A 14 ft piece with an 8 ft wind lever on a surface slab can tilt in a quake and keep settling after.

New building floors go at 12.7 ft NAVD88 or higher, for 36 in of sea level rise plus freeboard. Cityside Park was raised and now sits about 0.7 ft above the 100-year still water plus 36 in; planning to adapt it further starts once the Bay rises 30 in. The park is 300 ft wide partly to leave room for that work, so a permanent foundation there needs coordinating with TIDA.

All elevations are NAVD88 (TI Infrastructure Plan 2011, USGS lidar). Soil depths are island-wide ranges; no boring log for the park was found.

Why it matters

Stainless steel tea-stains where chlorides sit and nothing washes them away. Because the sculpture's top panel covers its whole depth, vertical rain never reaches any of the inside, where the mirror finish matters most. Only storm rain blown into the mouth reaches it.

It needs 316L or duplex stainless, AAMA 2605 or porcelain enamel colours, isolated fasteners and a washing schedule.

Why it matters

San Francisco's bird-safe code treats open space of 2 acres or more dominated by vegetation or open water as an Urban Bird Refuge. For buildings inside one, or less than 300 ft from one in an unobstructed line, lighting must be minimal and shielded and “No uplighting shall be used.” The pad is inside Cityside Park, about 275 ft from the Bay.

The rule is written for buildings, so a sculpture may not strictly count, but a reviewer would read it as the standard. The proposal drew two fixtures firing straight up into a mirror-lined shell. The mirror strips turn the beams around and send them out of the mouth, across the lawn at eye level and up into the sky.

The fix: drop the uplights. If the piece must be lit, use shielded fixtures aimed down onto the painted panels, 3000 K or warmer, dimmed late and on a timer, following DarkSky's five principles.
